"Original" = bottom-up over all consistent
  (hand1, hand2, remaining_prizes, score_diff)
with |hand1|=|hand2|=|prizes|=k for each round k — not only opening-reachable.

"Optimized" adds: relative-rank renormalization, symmetry, tighter sd
bounds / early forced wins. That is what fits in 36 KB / 3.6 MB / 35 GB.

Naive size formula (cold infosets only)
---------------------------------------
At remaining size k, without score_diff:
  #states(k) = C(N,k)^3

Sum over k is ~ 8^N / N^(3/2). Score_diff multiplies by a polynomial
(~O(N^2)). Peak term is around k = N/2.

"rough x sd (f32)" = sum_C^3 * (S/2+1) * 4 bytes (crude).
"their opt states" = goofspiel-nash optimized W-L count (discard-on-tie).

Why the counts differ
---------------------
1. Tie rule (biggest rule difference)
   - Ours: rollover — equal bids leave prizes pending; next bid is for the
     stack. Extra dimension: pending bitmask (multi-prize hot states).
   - Theirs: discard — tied prize goes to neither; no pending stack.
   So the games are not the same. Reachable sets differ (see table:
   sometimes ours is smaller, sometimes larger).

2. What is being counted
   - Ours / discard-reachable above: only states touched from the opening
     deal (lazy DP memo).
   - Their "original": every consistent (h1,h2,prizes,sd) for every k,
     whether or not it appears from the shuffled opening. That is why
     N=5 original (49,001) > discard-reachable (22,357).

3. Relative-rank compression
   Absolute ranks that never appear in either hand can be renumbered.
   They cite 10x+; our solver now does this too (N=5 memo ~3.5k vs
   their optimized 4.2k — same order; remaining gap is mostly rollover
   vs discard and reachable-only vs full enumeration).

4. Symmetry / sd pruning
   Both use hand swap symmetry (value flips sign) and |sd| > remaining
   ⇒ terminal. Their optimized pass is more aggressive (forced wins on
   largest remaining prizes, tighter sd bounds).

5. Cold vs hot representation
   They index primarily by "start of round k" sets of size k.
   We also memoize cold (unrevealed prize set) and hot (face-up pending)
   separately, so one physical position can contribute more than one
   packed key.


Implications for N=13
---------------------
- Full optimized discard-tie W–L table (published): ~12B states, ~35 GB.
- Unoptimized / naive: terabytes.
- Our rollover tables differ from their discard-tie optimized counts;
  rank-normalize is already in our solver.
- A 10–20 MB N=13 target only works with heavy compression, quantization,
  or not storing a dense full-N table (e.g. on-demand subgame solve,
  points-objective without sd, or shipping only small-N tables).
